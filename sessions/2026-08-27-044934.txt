  Yes to "implementable in OpenFOAM" — it already is, and it converges and beats

  But "says something new about the physics" was not yet earned, and I found
  why. Three concrete defects:

  1. The streak reservoir — the thing that makes the model novel — isn't 
     working. Peak k through the pre-transitional region is 5–10× below DNS in
     both solvers (x=100: 0.0008 vs 0.0047; x=150: 0.0006 vs 0.0060). k decays
     where the DNS grows. Without it the model is just a Re_v-threshold
     intermittency model, close to existing Menter/Langtry practice, with
     Walters–Cokljat's k_L as prior art for the two-reservoir idea.
  2. The objective was blind to k. It scored c_f, U, θ and free-stream decay
     only. So losing the reservoir cost nothing, and the fit bought c_f accuracy
     through a compensating error: no streak energy → Blasius-thin boundary
     layer → Re_v too high (500 vs DNS 417 at x=150) → transition fires ~100
     units early → Λ_c inflated to ~498 to compensate. The reported agreement
     with the classical 440 was absorbing that error.
  3. The OpenFOAM model was not the model that was fitted. nuL used √(γk) while
     fit-openfoam-coeffs.py fitted with √k. Pre-transition γ≈0.02, so the
     elliptic solver ran with the lift-up term ~7× weaker than calibrated.

  The structural fix is that the ω equation's strain production α·S² is the SST
  substitution for α·(ω/k)·P, and those agree only when ν_t = k/ω. This model
  gates the viscosity, ν_t = γk/ω, so the correct form carries a γ. Ungated,
  mean shear drives ω up in a region carrying no turbulence, which dissipates
  streak energy at the turbulent rate — that's the mechanism emptying the
  reservoir.

⏺ Sharpest result of the session so far, from the DNS directly:

  C_ℓ = ν_t / (√k δ₉₉) = 0.0026 ± 0.0001 — constant to 4 % across the whole
  pre-transitional region (x = 60→205), while ν_t itself triples and δ₉₉
  doubles. The streak eddy viscosity follows an ordinary outer mixing-length
  scaling on a length that is a fixed fraction of the boundary-layer thickness,
  with production peaking at y ≈ 0.37 δ₉₉. Once transition starts, the
  production peak collapses to y ≈ 0.02 δ₉₉ and the scaling stops applying.

  That pins down exactly why the reservoir stays thin. The portable closure caps
  its length at C_s√k/ω ≈ 0.57, which barely moves along the plate, where the
  DNS wants 0.14δ₉₉ growing 0.20 → 0.44. The result is a bootstrap — too little
  ν_L → too little production → too little k → too little ν_L. Term by term at x
  = 60, the model dissipates 2.0× what it produces where the DNS runs at 0.66×,
  so the streak energy decays over the stretch where the DNS triples it.

  δ₉₉ is non-local, so the fix has to be a local surrogate. Two candidates are
  now fitting as structural variants rather than being assumed: the
  wall-distance-limited mixing length (algebraic streak growth) and ν_L = C_L
  k/ω (exponential growth, equivalent to a floor on the activation γ). I also

⏺ Because calkit status is a staleness check, not a process monitor. It compares
  current dependency and output hashes against dvc.lock and lists what would
  need running. It has no concept of "in progress" — a stage that's
  mid-execution looks identical to one that hasn't started.

  What you see instead is the list silently shrinking. calkit run updates
  dvc.lock as each stage finishes, so completed stages just drop off. Right now:

  - Completed this run: build-paper, validate-bl-solver, discover-closure (plus
    the mesh sweep, laminar-sim, run-notebook from the earlier attempts)

  regime wants its own coefficients. That is the definition of a curve fit, and
  it's a clean, quantitative negative result for closing this with an algebraic
  relation in (k, S, y, ν).

  On the trivial solution — the 0.965 row is partly one, and I should not sell 
  it. Since −uv = R_uv·u′v′ and u′²/2k ≈ 0.8 is fairly steady, −uv/k ≈
  1.6·R_uv·(v′/u′). So regressing −uv/k on v′/u′ is close to tautological; it
  recovers the answer's own ingredients, exactly as you hit. The legitimate
  content is narrower: the target is essentially set by the anisotropy, and
  mean-field variables do not determine the anisotropy.

  fluctuation intensity — the thing that collapses in a streaky layer and

  measurement hints at.
