  On the flow it was built for, your closure beats Launder–Sharma 4.4×. On an
  equilibrium turbulent layer it never saw, the advantage inverts and its
  skin-friction error is 2.7× worse. It's still better on k (0.24 vs 0.48

  Grid-converged: across four grids from 121×200 to 361×700 the ordering never
  changes.

  That's the paper's thesis measured rather than asserted, and it cost no new
  data — the Jiménez profiles were sitting in data/jiminez/ being used only for
  a-priori regression.

  existing script, which is how they stayed broken. Fixed.

  - Edeling et al. 2014 is close prior art. Bayesian k-ε coefficient variability
    across boundary-layer flows, with posteriors, showing no single set is
    supported by all. Our non-transferability claim needs softening; what's ours
    is the three-routes-failing-identically demonstration and the harness.
  - The harness gap looks real. McConkey et al. is a curated dataset for
    a-priori work; I found no open harness that runs a supplied closure
    a-posteriori across a case suite. Section 6 flags this as targeted rather
    than exhaustive search — re-check before claiming it in print.

  Your two questions, answered in ideas-log §7.1/§7.2:
  - The momentum-equation term library genuinely hasn't been tried — the three
    things that look like it aren't it. But it'll fit beautifully and not
    transfer; the closure-level version already gets R²=0.953 in-sample and −472
    on Jiménez. Callaham et al. is the constructive angle: your
    coeff_disagreement = 1.0 between regions is a statement that different terms
    dominate in different places, so identify balance regimes first.
  - On Bayesian optimization your suspicion is right about the optimizer and
    wrong about where the value is. §4.14 measured search noise at 1.1 against
    structure differences of 0.04 — BO converges faster to an unidentified
    optimum. The prize is the posterior width: per-case posteriors whose overlap
    is a calibrated transferability measure.

⏺ Done. Answering your question first, then what changed.

  Is all-stats.h5 used for validating models? No.

  It has 743,680 rows × 59 columns. 36 of those columns — every single one that 
  needs pyJHTDB — are populated at 100 rows. That's 0.013%. And nothing reads
  them: plot-bl-dns.py and the three scripts under sim/ use only y and u between
  them.

  Worse, extract-jhtdb-stats never called make_stats(). It called read_stats(),
  which gdown-downloads a prebuilt file from Google Drive if one is missing. So
  the "extract" stage was a no-op guard around an opaque fetch, and it put a
  token-gated dependency on the critical path of a pipeline that never used it.

  So I did what you suggested, plus one step further: rather than just
  decoupling make_stats, the stage now derives the table from the tracked
  time-ave-profiles.h5. It's bit-identical to the shipped file on all 23 columns
  that have a consumer, needs no token, and is genuinely reproducible instead
  of a link only you can regenerate. fetch_sampled_gradients (ex-make_stats)
  moved to scripts/standalone/, which has a README stating that nothing in the
  pipeline may depend on anything in it.

  Environments

  which is what actually buys the isolation:

  ├────────┼──────────────┼──────────┼───────────────┤

  Two things I'd flag

  Two findings:

  The clip closures cannot reach steady state in a channel. Not slow — they
  don't converge, and the answer drifts with the pseudo-step, while
  Launder–Sharma lands on an identical answer for pseudo-steps spanning a factor
  of 800 (Ub_err = 0.0527 at dx = 0.25, 4, 50). So the method is right and the
  closures are the problem. This is the third independent sighting of ideas-log 
  §4.3 — "a hard rectifier makes cells toggle across the threshold", which
  stalled the OpenFOAM run at its iteration limit. Different solver, different
  geometry, same defect.

  I made the harness refuse to score an unconverged run (no-converge, not a
  number). A score read off a state that's still moving isn't a result.

  clip-k-omega-gamma, the project's headline model, has a transfer penalty of 
  5.8. The simpler clip-k-gamma has 1.11. The elaborate one is best in-sample
  and worst at leaving home.

  Caveat I'd want checked before it goes in a paper: Launder–Sharma scores
  identically to laminar at Re_τ=180 (127.9 vs 138.4) — total relaminarization.
  That's a known low-Re k-ε weakness, but identical-to-laminar is suspicious
  enough that I'd verify it's not an artifact of my initialization before
  quoting it.

  Separated external flow: I found something better than a cylinder

  The KTH FLOW / FAU database has well-resolved LES of a NACA 4412 wing section
  (Vinuesa et al. 2018) — 50 stations, x/c = 0.153→0.983, at Re_c = 100k, 200k,
  400k and 1M, each carrying U, V, Ue, u_τ, c_f, δ99, θ, β and the Reynolds
  stresses. Downloaded and characterized.

  Why it beats a cylinder for your purpose:

  - External flow on an aerofoil — what the models are actually used for.
  - β reaches 112 at Re_c=400k and 1720 at Re_c=100k, against β ~ 1–4 in the
    Bobke flat-plate APG data. Far more severe.
  - c_f falls to 1.8×10⁻⁴ and never goes negative. On the verge of separating,
    never separates.

  That third point is the one that matters architecturally. The parabolic
  boundary-layer equations have the Goldstein singularity at c_f = 0 — a
  marching solver cannot pass a separation point. That's mathematics, not an
  implementation limit, so genuinely separated flow is Tier 2 by necessity. But
  a near-separation case that stays attached is the hardest thing the fast
  screen can be asked to do, and near-separation is precisely what decides
  whether a model calls stall right.

  A cylinder at Re=3900 would be worse for you: massively separated (Tier 2
  only), and the separation is essentially laminar, so it doesn't test turbulent
  BL separation at all.
