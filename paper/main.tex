\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

% TODO (human): skeleton. The framing has changed since the first draft: the
% goal is now a closure whose single coefficient set holds across every
% reference case, and the deliverable is as much the benchmark as the model.

A RANS closure is only useful with one set of coefficients. Tuning per case
is not modeling; it is interpolation, and the literature on data-driven
closures \citep{Duraisamy2019, Brunton2020, SandbergZhao2022} is largely a
record of models that were excellent on the flow they were built from and
untested, or worse than their baseline, elsewhere \citep{Nishi2024,
RumseyColemanWang2022}. That this is not a machine-learning problem but a
calibration problem has been known since damping functions were first fitted
to DNS budgets \citep{RodiMansour1993} and since coefficient posteriors were
first shown to move between boundary-layer flows \citep{Edeling2014}. What
has been missing is the routine test: a suite of reference cases with
declared targets, and a scoring rule that refuses to average the flows a
model was fitted on with the flows it was not.

This paper does three things. It assembles such a suite
(\S\ref{sec:cases}): \finding[benchmark_n_cases] DNS and LES cases in five
geometries, from the bypass-transitional plate to a wall-free mixing layer,
each with provenance recorded and its scored quantities and targets declared.
It develops a transition closure on one of them (\S\ref{sec:closure}) and
tests it, with the same coefficients, on all of them alongside published
models run under identical setups (\S\ref{sec:models},
\S\ref{sec:results}). And it describes the benchmarking harness
(\S\ref{sec:gym}) as a reusable, forkable project: adding a closure or a case
is a registration, and every number in this paper is regenerated by its
pipeline.

The headline result is negative and, we argue, the point. The closure that
matches the calibration DNS best is the worst-transferring model in the
suite, and textbook Launder--Sharma, with no coefficient fitted here, is the
best model out of sample. Three independent routes --- a-posteriori structure
search, a-priori term regression, and an integral flux budget --- each
produce excellent in-sample agreement and each fail to transfer, and the
diagnostics that normally accompany a data-driven closure would not have
told us. We report what does transfer, what the DNS says about the form a
closure must take, and what the benchmark now makes routine.

\section{Reference cases}\label{sec:cases}

Every case is a flow with reference data, a way to run a closure on it, and
a declared target error per scored quantity: the error at which a careful
reader would call the model and the data indistinguishable. Targets are the
most contestable thing in the benchmark, which is why each sits at the top of
its case definition rather than inside an objective function. Every case
except the first is out of sample for every closure in this paper.

\subsection{Bypass-transitional flat plate (calibration case)}

The primary dataset is the JHTDB \texttt{transition\_bl} DNS
\citep{JHTDBDescription, Zaki2013}: a flat plate under free-stream turbulence,
$x \in [\result[XInlet], \result[XOutlet]]$, $y \in [0, \result[YMax]]$, $\nu = 1.25\times10^{-3}$, with
$Tu = \result[TuInlet]\,\%$ and $\delta_{99} = \result[DeltaInlet]$ already developed at the inlet
station. Skin friction falls to a minimum at $x \approx 205$ and peaks near
$x \approx 450$. This is the only case any closure here was calibrated on.
Scored: skin friction, mean velocity, momentum thickness, free-stream decay
and turbulence energy.

\subsection{Zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layer}

Six stations of the DNS of \citet{Sillero2013} at $Re_\theta = 4000$--$6500$,
three to five times anything the calibration case reaches. The streamwise
spacing is recovered from the momentum integral $d\theta/dx = c_f/2$, so
nothing is fitted; the first station seeds the transported scalars and is
excluded from scoring. Tests the fully turbulent log layer. Scored: $c_f$,
$U$, $\theta$, $H$ and $k$.

\subsection{Turbulent channel flow}

\citet{LeeMoser2015} at $Re_\tau = 180$, $1000$ and $5200$. A different
geometry from everything else in the suite: no free stream, no edge, no
streamwise development, so the case cannot be passed by getting a transition
location right, only by getting the near-wall balance and the log layer
right. With $\delta = u_\tau = 1$ the total stress is exactly linear and $U$
follows from one integration once the closure supplies $\nu_t$; the
transported scalars relax to a fixed point, and a closure that never reaches
one scores $\infty$ rather than a number read off a moving state. Scored:
$U^+$, bulk and centerline velocity, and $k$.

\subsection{NACA 4412 suction side}

The well-resolved LES of \citet{Vinuesa2018} at $Re_c = 4\times10^5$ and
$10^6$: external flow under a severe adverse pressure gradient, with the
Clauser parameter reaching $\beta = 112$ and $c_f$ falling to $1.8\times10^{-4}$
without separating. The only non-DNS reference in the suite, and the
resolved stresses omit the sub-grid share, which the case records. The
parabolic equations carry the Goldstein singularity at separation, so this is
as close to separation as the fast tier can go. Scored: $c_f$, $U$, $\theta$
and, near the trailing edge, absolute $c_f$ and the shape factor.

\subsection{Temporally evolving mixing layer}

The DNS of \citet{Lusher2026} from the JAXA DNS database
\citep{JAXADNSDatabase}: a tanh shear layer at $\Delta U L_x/\nu = 250{,}000$
that is turbulent almost immediately, thickens as a plane mixing layer, then
rolls up into vortices \citep{Sansica2026}. Scored over the one-dimensional
phase $\hat t \in [0.14, 0.40]$, before roll-up, on the momentum-thickness
growth and the peak shear stress and kinetic energy, with every closure
seeded by the reference's own initial condition. The first case in the suite
with no wall.

\section{Models tested and simulation setup}\label{sec:models}\label{sec:method}

% TODO (human): skeleton. Every model, with the coefficients it was run with

% comparison. Nothing here is tuned per case.

\subsection{Models}

Seven closures run in the Python tier and five in OpenFOAM. In both: a
laminar (no-model) lower bound; low-Reynolds-number $k$--$\epsilon$ of
\citet{LaunderSharma1974} with published coefficients; and the transported
$k$--$\omega$--$\gamma$ clipping closure developed here (\S\ref{sec:closure}),
with the \result[NFittedCoeffs]{} coefficients fitted once on the
calibration case and never changed. The Python tier adds four structural
variants of the clipping closure, kept because their failures are
informative: an algebraic mixing-length form, a transported-$k$ form, a
two-reservoir streak/active split, and an entropy-carrying variant. The
OpenFOAM tier adds the published transition baselines: $k$--$\omega$ SST
\citep{Menter2006}, the Langtry--Menter $\gamma$--$Re_{\theta t}$ model
\citep{LangtryMenter2009}, and the Walters--Cokljat $k_L$--$k_T$--$\omega$
model \citep{Walters2008}, whose laminar kinetic energy is the closest
published relative of the streak reservoir in ours.

\subsection{Solvers}

Two fidelities are used. Structure and coefficient searches are screened with
a parabolic boundary-layer marching solver, which advances the mean momentum
and closure equations downstream from the DNS inlet profile in about a second
per candidate. Every result quoted for a model is then reproduced in
OpenFOAM v2412 (\texttt{simpleFoam}, steady SIMPLE) on a wall-resolved mesh,
because the parabolic solver is optimistic: it is handed the inlet profile,
marches without an elliptic pressure field, and returned roughly a factor of
three lower skin-friction error than the elliptic solve for the same closure
and coefficients. Its discretization error is known: against the Blasius
similarity solution it reproduces the skin friction to within
\finding[screening_solver_cf_error_pct]\,\%, well below the differences
between the models it is used to rank, and the bias is systematic, so
comparisons between closures stay fair.

The elliptic domain is a single block matched to the DNS extent,
$x \in [\result[XInlet], \result[XOutlet]]$ and $y \in [0, \result[YMax]]$,
with 80 cells in $y$ graded to a first cell at $y^+ \lesssim 1$. Placing the
inlet at the DNS station rather than upstream of a leading edge is not
cosmetic; see \S\ref{sec:bench}.

\subsection{Inlet and initial conditions}

Every model is given the measured DNS profile of $U$ and $k$ at the inlet
station. The inlet $\omega$ cannot be shared, because under the analytic
free-decay law $k \propto (1 + \beta \omega_0 (x-x_{\rm in})/U)^{-\beta^*/\beta}$
the decay exponent is $\beta^*/\beta$; a single $\omega_0$ would give models
with different $\beta$ different free-stream decay. It is therefore fitted per
model to the same measured decay just downstream of the inlet. For the
$\gamma$--$Re_{\theta t}$ model the inlet $Re_{\theta t}$ is taken from that
model's own empirical correlation at the inlet turbulence intensity, giving
$\result[ReThetatInlet]$.

On cases whose inlet is already turbulent, every closure is seeded from the
reference data by one rule --- $k$ from the measured stresses, $\omega$ from
the measured eddy viscosity $-\langle u'v'\rangle/(dU/dy)$, $\gamma = 1$ ---
and the seeded station or transient is excluded from scoring. Without this
the channel case measured whether a low-Reynolds-number model can self-start
from $k \approx 0$, which is a fixed point of every closure here, rather than
whether it sustains the right turbulence; the same closure scored $128$
unseeded and $3.3$ seeded. The seed is a property of the flow, supplied by
the case, so no closure has to know about any case
\citep[cf.][\S3.1]{Sansica2026}.

Transition-model comparisons are dominated by case setup. The
$\gamma$--$Re_{\theta t}$ model \citep{LangtryMenter2009} was run on the same

\section{The closure developed here}\label{sec:closure}

\subsection{Form and coefficients}

The closure under test transports $k$, $\omega$ and an activation fraction
$\gamma$, with the eddy viscosity gated as $\nu_t = \gamma k/\omega$ and the
$\gamma$ source rectified about a threshold in the vorticity Reynolds number.
It has \result[NFittedCoeffs]{} fitted coefficients.

Fitting is by random search over the coefficient bounds
(\result[NRandom]{} samples) followed by \result[NRefineRounds]{} rounds of
Gaussian refinement about the incumbent (\result[NRefine]{} samples each, with
successively shrinking width). The objective is a weighted sum of relative
errors in skin friction, mean velocity, momentum thickness, free-stream decay
and turbulence energy, each divided by a target value so that a contribution
of one means that quantity has arrived. The turbulence-energy term is included
because an objective built only on the mean field cannot see a closure that
carries an order of magnitude too little $k$.

\subsection{What the DNS says about the form}\label{sec:structure}

A scalar eddy viscosity assumes the Reynolds-stress anisotropy and the mean
strain share an eigenframe. In the DNS they are misaligned by
\finding[misalignment_deg_pretransition]$^\circ$ before transition and still
by \finding[misalignment_deg_turbulent]$^\circ$ in equilibrium turbulence
(equivalently a tensor-anisotropy match $\cos 2\alpha$ of about $0.03$ and
$0.7$), so an eddy viscosity is structurally wrong in the pre-transitional
region and imperfect everywhere. This bounds any eddy-viscosity closure,
including the one below.

Transition is a change of correlation, not of energy partition. Decomposing
the structure parameter into a coherence and a partition, the $u$--$v$
correlation coefficient grows \finding[coherence_ratio]$\times$ through
transition while the partition changes by
\finding[anisotropy_ratio]$\times$; the information shared between the
velocity components grows from \finding[total_correlation_bits_laminar] to
\finding[total_correlation_bits_turbulent] bits, a factor of
\finding[total_correlation_ratio].

The component entropy of the partition is non-monotone, falling from above
unity to a minimum of \finding[entropy_min] at $x = \finding[entropy_min_x]$
before recovering, so a constitutive relation in entropy alone is hysteretic
rather than single-valued. Implemented as a closure it does not hold the
laminar state (Appendix~\ref{sec:questions}, Q6).

Thermodynamically, the turbulence acts as a buffer between exergy injection
at the wall and entropy rejection: the stored fraction of the energy routed
into turbulence peaks at \finding[peak_turbulent_storage_pct]\,\% at
$x = \finding[peak_turbulent_storage_x]$, just after onset, then collapses to
a few percent, and the turbulent share of entropy rejection rises from
\finding[turbulent_rejection_fraction_low_Re] to
\finding[turbulent_rejection_fraction_high_Re] across the Reynolds numbers
of the turbulent region.

\subsection{Which terms carry the closure}\label{sec:ablation}

Removing one ingredient at a time from the fitted closure and re-scoring in
the parabolic solver: the rectifier and the lift-up term are load-bearing,
and two others are not. With the rectifier removed the skin-friction error
rises from \finding[ablation_reference_cf_pct]\,\% to
\finding[ablation_no_clip_cf_pct]\,\%, worse than $k$--$\epsilon$ at
\finding[baseline_k_epsilon_cf_pct]\,\%; without the lift-up term it is
\finding[ablation_no_liftup_cf_pct]\,\%. The streak viscous-decay term and
the gate on $\omega$ production change the score by nothing and can be
deleted. Among the transition-gate drivers tested, every variant of the
vorticity Reynolds number $Re_v = y^2 \Omega / \nu$ beats every other
driver, and the fitted threshold $\Lambda_c = \finding[fitted_threshold_Lambda_c]$
lies near the classical $440$; but the model is sensitive to it, since
imposing $440$ raises the error to
\finding[ablation_classical_threshold_cf_pct]\,\%, and
\S\ref{sec:fails} shows the fit wanders within search noise.

An evolutionary search over the structure of the activation source, free to
choose smooth responses, is not yet evidence either way: after the pipeline
was re-run its top eight structures use no rectifier, where an earlier run's
all did, and the stage does not reproduce from run to run. A converged,
reproducible search is owed before any structural claim rests on it.

\section{The benchmark}\label{sec:gym}

% TODO (human): skeleton. docs/rans-gym.md sections 1-3 give the reasoning.

The harness scores every registered closure on every registered case,
a posteriori --- actually run in a solver --- rather than against a table of
features and labels, because a-priori accuracy does not imply a-posteriori
accuracy \citep{Duraisamy2021, Zhao2020} and \S\ref{sec:fails} reproduces
that in-house. A closure is one class implementing three methods
(initialize, eddy viscosity, advance); a case owns how to run a closure on
its flow, what the reference data are, and what error counts as matching
them. Both are registered, and third-party closures or cases are picked up
from a named module without editing the package.

\subsection{Scoring across cases}\label{sec:scoring}

A case $c$ declares, for each metric $m$ it scores, a target error
$\tau_{c,m}$; the normalized score of closure $\mathcal{M}$ on case $c$ is
%
\begin{equation}
  s_c(\mathcal{M}) = \frac{1}{|M_c|} \sum_{m \in M_c}
    \frac{e_{c,m}(\mathcal{M})}{\tau_{c,m}},
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
%
so $s_c = 1$ means the closure matches the data to within the declared target
on every metric. Each closure declares the set $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M})$ of
cases its coefficients were fitted on, and the leaderboard reports
%
\begin{equation}
  \bar{s}_{\mathrm{in}} = \langle s_c \rangle_{c \in \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M})},
  \qquad
  \bar{s}_{\mathrm{out}} = \langle s_c \rangle_{c \notin \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M})},
  \qquad
  \pi = \bar{s}_{\mathrm{out}} / \bar{s}_{\mathrm{in}},
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
%
with the transfer penalty $\pi$ the number this paper is about. A run that
diverges or fails to converge scores $s_c = \infty$.

\subsection{A-priori regression as a diagnostic}

Candidate closure terms are evaluated directly from the DNS and their
coefficients solved for by least squares, rather than being scored after a
forward solve. The target is normalised by $k$, making it the structure
parameter and the library dimensionless; an unnormalised target spans orders
of magnitude across the plate and the fit would see only the turbulent end.

Two guards are applied. The library admits only quantities a RANS solver
possesses, so that no column carries information from the target, and the
correlation of every column with the target is reported so that a
near-identity is visible rather than being read as a result. Fits are then
evaluated on data not used to produce them: the opposite half of the plate,
and the independent DNS.

\subsection{Reproducibility and the living project}

Every number and figure in this paper is produced by a pipeline stage from
version-controlled inputs, and the values quoted in the text are generated
from the results files rather than transcribed. The questions the project
set out to answer, their answers, and the evidence behind each are recorded
in the project metadata and rendered in Appendix~\ref{sec:questions} from
the same results files; a check reports any answer whose cited evidence has
changed since it was last edited.

The benchmark is meant to be forked rather than extended in place. A
derived project registers a new closure or case, re-runs the pipeline, and
regenerates this document with its own results; its metadata records the
project it was derived from, so every leaderboard traces to the suite and
the scoring rule it inherited.

The closure with the best in-sample score is the worst-transferring of the
models that run, and textbook Launder--Sharma, with no coefficient fitted

The mixing layer separates the closure family by construction. The three
closures whose length scale is a van Driest-damped mixing length produce no
turbulence at all without a wall and score identically to laminar, while the
transported-$\omega$ variant, whose length scale is $k/\omega$, is the best
model on the case at \finding[mixing_layer_best_normalized] (momentum
thickness within \finding[mixing_layer_clip_k_omega_gamma_dtheta_pct]\,\%),
ahead of Launder--Sharma at
\finding[mixing_layer_launder_sharma_normalized]. Any closure meant to
leave the plate needs a length scale made of transported quantities.


\subsection{What transfers}\label{sec:transfers}

Two measured quantities survive cross-validation between the two DNS.

The structure parameter $a_1 = -\langle u'v'\rangle / 2k$, integrated across
the layer, is $\result[aOneTransitional]$ at the downstream end of the transitional case and
$\result[aOneJimenez] \pm \result[aOneJimenezSd]$ across $Re_\theta = 4000$--$6500$ in the independent DNS
--- agreement to \result[aOneRelDiff]\,\% across a fourfold change in $Re_\theta$. This is
Bradshaw's structure parameter \citep{Bradshaw1967} and is not new; it is
reported here because it is the only quantity in this study that behaves like
a constant.

The dissipation coefficient $C_\epsilon = \epsilon L / u'^3$, with
$L = \delta_{99}$ and $u' = \sqrt{2\bar{k}/3}$, is obtained without modelling
from the exact integral balance
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_{\rm int} = P_{\rm int} - \frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}
  \int U k \, {\rm d}y ,
  \label{eq:intbudget}
\end{equation}
in which the transport terms are internal and integrate to vanishing boundary
fluxes. It is $\result[CepsTurb] \pm \result[CepsTurbSd]$ over $Re_\theta = 900$--$1500$ in the
transitional case and $\result[CepsJimenez]$ in the independent DNS.
 Through transition it varies by a factor of \result[CepsRatio],
rising from $\result[CepsPre]$; the ratio $P/\epsilon$ peaks at $\result[PoverEpsPeak]$ near
$x \approx \result[PoverEpsPeakX]$, coinciding with the peak in turbulent energy storage
($x \approx 246$) and the minimum of the component entropy of the fluctuation
field ($x \approx 233$). That the cascade is out of equilibrium through
transition is established \citep{Vassilicos2015, LiuFangFang2021}; the
measurement is included as validation of the budget, not as a new result.

\subsection{What does not transfer}\label{sec:fails}

Regressing $-\langle u'v'\rangle/k$ on a library of \result[NTerms] dimensionless groups
formed only from quantities a RANS solver possesses ($\partial U/\partial y$,
$\partial^2 U/\partial y^2$, $y$, $\nu$, $k$) gives $R^2 = \result[StressInSample]$ in sample.
Coefficients fitted downstream of $x = 450$ and applied upstream give
$R^2 = \result[StressDownToUp]$; coefficients fitted on the transitional DNS and applied to the
independent DNS give $R^2 = \result[StressToJimenez]$, although the latter fits itself to
$R^2 = \result[JimenezSelfFit]$. The normalised disagreement between upstream and downstream
coefficient vectors is $\result[StressCoeffDisagree]$, its maximum. The same holds for the unclosed
residual of the $k$ budget: $R^2 = \result[EpsInSample]$ in sample, $\result[EpsUpToDown]$ and $\result[EpsDownToUp]$ out.

Every regime is separately and easily fittable; no coefficient set serves two
of them.

\subsubsection*{Trivial solutions}

Two failure modes produce high scores with no content.

A library column carrying information from the target reconstructs it. With
$-\langle u'v'\rangle = R_{uv} u' v'$ and $u'^2/2k$ approximately steady,
$-\langle u'v'\rangle/k$ is nearly proportional to $v'/u'$, so including
$v'/u'$ --- unavailable to a RANS solver in any case --- raised $R^2$ from
$0.361$ to $0.965$ on an impoverished four-term library. On the \result[NTerms]-term
library the same addition moves $\result[StressInSample]$ to $\result[StressDiagnostic]$, showing the first result
measured library poverty rather than missing physics.

Monotonic variables correlate with monotonic targets. $C_\epsilon$ correlates
with the activation $\gamma = a_1/a_{1\infty}$ at $r = \result[CollapseGammaCorr]$, yet collapses
onto it no better than $\result[CollapseGamma]\,\%$ relative RMS --- worse than onto the
streamwise coordinate $x$ itself ($\result[CollapseX]\,\%$), which carries no physical
content. Correlation coefficients are not evidence of a closure relation; a
trivial-coordinate baseline should be reported alongside any claimed collapse.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conc}

% TODO (human): rewrite for the new framing.

A closure has to hold with one coefficient set across flows, and the only
way to know whether it does is to test it on flows it was not fitted to,
under a rule that never averages the two. Under that rule the closure
developed here is the best model on its calibration case and the
worst-transferring one in the suite, and textbook Launder--Sharma is the
best model out of sample. In-sample agreement, correlation coefficients and
structure rankings all failed to distinguish a transferable relation from a
case-specific fit; the diagnostics that did discriminate were out-of-sample
transfer, a trivial-coordinate baseline for claimed collapses, and repetition
of the inner fit under different random seeds. The benchmark makes all three
routine, and it is built to be forked.
